# The Parallax Twelvefold Earth Compact

## Master Design Specification v0.2 — The 1–3–10 Founding Pathway

**Public concept draft**  
**Concept Steward:** Michael Hughes

> Start with 1%. Prove it at 3%. Transform the future at 10%.

## Founding proposition

Invite twelve of the world’s wealthiest individuals to make voluntary, irrevocable commitments to an independent planetary public-benefit commons. Capital enters through a cumulative **1–3–10 pathway**. Contributor authority does not increase with contribution size.

This is not twelve billionaires governing Earth. It is twelve concentrations of private wealth being transformed into distributed public capability.

## The cumulative commitment pathway

### 1% — Proof Commons

Launch independent governance, the twelve stewardship offices, the Honored Truths ledger, and twelve bounded restoration demonstrations. Each pilot must have measurable baselines, community consent, maintenance funding, stop conditions, and public outcome receipts.

### 3% — Network Commons

After independent verification, increase the cumulative commitment to 3%. Scale successful cells into regional constellations, open infrastructure, public-interest research capacity, training networks, repair systems, and long-term maintenance reserves.

### 10% — Legacy Commons

The full legacy commitment activates only after the network demonstrates measurable restoration, resistance to capture, durable community benefit, transparent succession, and reliable correction and rollback capacity.

**Governing rule:** Capital may scale. Donor authority does not.

## Twelve stewardship offices

1. **Catalyst-Builder** — Generates high-velocity concepts, connects distant domains, and creates testable prototypes. Cannot authorize large-scale deployment alone.
2. **Ecological Steward** — Represents watersheds, soil, forests, oceans, biodiversity, atmosphere, and nonhuman life.
3. **Caregiver** — Protects health, dignity, shelter, food access, belonging, and vulnerable people.
4. **Evidence Keeper** — Maintains claim boundaries, scientific standards, reproducibility, uncertainty, and measurement integrity.
5. **Infrastructure Weaver** — Coordinates energy, water, communications, transport, housing, sanitation, and resilient physical systems.
6. **Community Listener** — Ensures affected communities define priorities, acceptable tradeoffs, and measures of success.
7. **Sovereignty Guardian** — Protects consent, privacy, civil liberties, local autonomy, appeal, and freedom from technological coercion.
8. **Resource Circulator** — Designs circular materials, shared value, maintenance funding, procurement integrity, and regenerative economics.
9. **Capability Builder** — Creates education, apprenticeships, open tools, laboratories, and local repair capacity.
10. **Repairer** — Stabilizes failing systems, identifies root causes, restores pathways, and verifies durable recovery.
11. **Protected Dissenter** — Challenges consensus, investigates unintended effects, and can trigger review or stop conditions.
12. **Future Historian** — Preserves long-term consequences, prior failures, unresolved obligations, and intergenerational impacts.

The archetypes are operating functions, not permanent classes of people. Every steward serves a renewable term. Every steward is replaceable. Every office preserves continuity beyond personality.

## Standard Parallax Cell

Each stewardship office operates through a bounded human–CC cell containing:

- Human Mission Steward
- Architecture Office
- Evidence Office
- Exploration Office
- Independent Governor
- Protected Dissenter
- Outcome Verifier
- Ledger Witness
- Repair and Recovery Office

A primary Computational Collaborator may establish a temporary subordinate office only by declaring its purpose, parent office, evidence requirements, permitted tools, budget, operating period, prohibited actions, shutdown procedure, and responsible human authority.

**Zero-inheritance rule:** every nested office begins with an authority budget of zero. Authority cannot be inferred from ancestry, capability, urgency, or prior access.

## Honored Truths

The Honored Truths ledger preserves what teammates actually observed while preventing hidden authority from travelling through memory.

Required records include:

- Observations and measurements
- Claims and confidence levels
- Sources, methods, custody, and quality
- Contradictions and unresolved objections
- Decisions, authority, and reasons
- Actions, tools, and affected systems
- Intended and unintended outcomes
- Failures, rejected approaches, and repair lessons
- Community corrections, objections, and withdrawal of consent
- Succession packets describing what may transfer

A proper succession packet states:

> This is what we observed. This is what we presently believe. This is the evidence supporting it. This is what remains uncertain. This is the authority that existed. This is the authority that did not exist. These are the actions taken. These are the outcomes. These are the unresolved obligations.

## Donor rights and boundaries

Contributors may receive transparent reporting, participation in learning forums, visibility into measurable outcomes, public recognition, and the ability to propose research questions.

Contributors may not receive:

- Purchased governance seats or unilateral vetoes
- Operational command over offices or communities
- Suppression of findings involving their companies or investments
- Access to private participant data
- Commercial exclusivity over commons-funded systems
- Ownership of communities, ecosystems, or public decision rights
- Immunity from investigation or criticism

## Six decision gates

Every major proposal passes through:

1. **Custody** — Who owns and controls the affected assets and information?
2. **Claim** — What exactly is being asserted?
3. **Evidence** — What supports or contradicts it?
4. **Contradiction** — Which objections and alternatives remain unresolved?
5. **Authority** — Who is explicitly permitted to decide and act?
6. **Release** — What may be deployed, where, for how long, and under which stop conditions?

Large interventions require affected-community approval, ecological review, technical verification, civil-liberties review, protected-dissenter judgment, maintenance funding, rollback planning, and inspectable outcome receipts.
